Build overview

Elemental attack crit, one fast claw and a shield. Use regular Lightning Strike as the main skill, adding Vaal LS if found. Barkskin from Mother's Teachings and Lesson of the Seasons anchors the defenses, alongside freeze control from Oath of Winter, life-on-hit plus life/mana leech, elemental resistance caps, suppression, evasion and a useful shield. Unbound Avatar provides a later manual damage window. One tincture is optional burst damage; the build does not depend on tincture uptime.

Opening: take only the Quickstep arc, not both parallel arcs. The strike mastery arrives at spent point 25; pierce follows, then Clever Thief for both life and mana leech. The 90-point core contains 123% increased maximum Life and +30 flat life from passives.

Passive point budget. Each level after level 1 grants one character passive point. The campaign grants 23 more with Alira or 24 if all bandits are killed. Use /passives to check quest rewards. The route has a 90-point core, a 103-point mapping milestone and a 122-point endgame target. Labyrinth grants its own ascendancy points. Quest point list

Suppression: The completed character core supplies 80% unconditional chance; the two Barkskin connecting passives add 10%, for 90% before gear. The selected Evasion Mastery adds 15% only with evasion on helmet, body, gloves and boots, reaching 105% total (capped at 100%). Without that mastery condition, find another 10% from gear. Before both Barkskin connectors are allocated, budget for the missing 5% or 10%. Check away from full life, because Revenge of the Hunted can hide a gap while full.

Joining the route mid-campaign or in maps. Use the progression menu to find the milestone closest to your spent points, then fill in missing core nodes before taking later branches. To switch from a tincture-focused tree, refund its masteries and branch tips first, keeping any travel nodes shared with the core. See the Warden section for the ascendancy respec order.

Swapping early passives. If you started through One With Nature, connect the Quickstep path and cover any lost resistances on gear before refunding the branch. If you took Essence Sap for mana, test your sustain with Clever Thief before refunding Essence Sap. Keep any travel nodes used by other branches.

Use a shield for SSF maps. Put your stronger claw in the main hand and look for a rare evasion or armour/evasion shield with life and the resistances or suppression you're missing. A shield adds attack block and enables Shield Charge. Lightning Strike alternates weapons when dual wielding, so a weak second claw contributes weaker hits. Adjust the last points to your equipment

  • Accuracy: → → is a three-point Depth Perception branch from (46277). Use it instead of optional crit damage if needed. Check hit chance against the relevant enemy level; do not assume a low-level tooltip proves endgame accuracy.
  • A good jewel: → is two points from (20807). Take it when the jewel is worth both points. This replaces another optional damage investment; no specific unique jewel is required.
  • Temporary early mana/attributes: → is a two-point Primal Spirit branch from Finesse. It helps Strength/Intelligence and mana on hit after using a mana flask. Use it if early gear fails those needs, then refund once gear and Clever Thief replace its purpose.
  • Point Blank remains optional: → costs two points from (30679). It affects projectile distance scaling, not the melee component, and is not 30% more total LS damage. Compare positioning and returning-projectile travel before allocating it.
  • Replace a function, then refund it: actual gear-based strike targets can replace the attack mastery; enough actual gear-based pierce can replace the three-point pierce branch. Do not remove a function simply because the desired item might drop later.
  • After the 103-point target: spend earned points on a proven jewel, missing life/accuracy, needed attributes or damage checked against your equipment. Cluster/timeless jewels and unusual uniques are optional projects, not assumptions in this plan.

Start here: your next upgrade, one act at a time

This is a Ranger — Lightning Strike Warden SSF route for the normal campaign (not Ruthless). Begin with Frost Blades, then switch when Lightning Strike feels better. All life and gear targets below are practical recommendations, not quest requirements or guarantees; stop to fix a weak defense if dying repeatedly.

Your Act 1 priorities: take Frost Blades, get the Quicksilver Flask, use a fast weapon with a shield, find a useful 3-link, and collect the two early passive books. Keep a mana flask. You do not need a unique item, a perfect claw or a tincture yet.

Reward vs purchase: “Take” means a free quest selection; “buy” means vendor currency. Choose only one reward per selection, then buy any missing alternatives later. Random rare rewards are not guaranteed upgrades. Consume passive books immediately. Use the passive map to match the route to your current spent points.

How Barkskin works: enable the level-20 skill granted by Mother’s Teachings. It requires no skill gem, socket or mana reservation. Maximum Bark is 10; gain 2 Bark each second if you have not been hit by an enemy attack Recently. Enemy attack hits remove one Bark. At high Bark, gain flat physical attack-hit reduction; as Bark falls, gain more chance to evade attacks. Lesson of the Seasons makes enemy spell-damage hits remove Bark too, adds flat reduction to each damage type in spell hits, and increases the amount of suppressed spell damage prevented as Bark falls. Spell hits do not change the skill’s attack-hit condition for regaining Bark.

3.30 values: Barkskin grants -75 physical damage taken from attack hits per Bark and 6% more chance to evade attacks per missing Bark. Lesson grants -40 damage taken of each damage type from spell hits per Bark and +3% suppressed spell damage prevented per missing Bark. At 10 Bark, that is -750 physical attack damage and -400 of each damage type from spell hits; at zero Bark, the suppression benefit is +30 percentage points of prevention when a spell is suppressed. These are different states, not simultaneous maximum benefits. More evade chance is not added percentage points, and evasion still has its normal cap.

Suppression still matters: the two small Barkskin passives add 10% chance. The completed character core plus these passives gives 90%; the conditional 15% Evasion Mastery caps it while all four armor slots have evasion. Without that condition, obtain at least 10% on gear. 1. An elemental claw good enough for early maps First priority

Bring: a spare fast claw, a few Screaming-or-better Essences of Wrath, Hatred or Anger, and currency for one finishing craft. Noble Claw is usable before Imperial Claw; Imperial requires character level 68 and has 1.6 base attacks/sec with life on hit. Item level 63+ is a useful early project, not a requirement to enter T1.

  1. Choose the guaranteed damage. Wrath gives flat lightning on a claw; Hatred gives cold; Anger gives fire. Read the claw/one-handed weapon line. Torment's spell lightning is not the same thing. Screaming and higher can repeatedly reforge a rare; lower essences need a normal item and may restrict other affix levels.
  2. Try one essence, then inspect. Look for a second worthwhile flat elemental prefix, local attack speed, and room to fix one missing stat. Cold somewhere in the attack supports the Warden freeze plan. Do not apply the next essence automatically.
  3. Choose the finishing branch. Two useful elements with no speed? Craft attack speed into an open suffix. Good speed plus two elements and an open prefix? Craft a third element you do not already have. A natural modifier usually blocks the bench version of its own mod family; you cannot overwrite a bad natural fire roll with crafted fire.
  4. Use the affordable unlocked craft. Check the bench's actual tier, recipe and price. One solid crafted stat is enough. If all relevant slots are full, keep a usable result or roll the spare again; an early Annul is not a reliable way to rescue it.
  5. Compare, equip, then stop. Count flat weapon damage and local attacks/sec, then check accuracy, crit, recovery and real boss sustain. Save your old claw before changing anything else.

2. A claw when you have Alterations instead of essences Alternative

Bring: an ordinary spare claw, Transmutation/Alteration/Augmentation Orbs, one Regal, and an unlocked flat-element bench craft. This can be slower than essences; choose it when those are the resources you already have.

  1. Start with a normal claw, or Scour an expendable ordinary rare. Transmute it. A magic claw can have only one prefix and one suffix, so do not search for two different flat elemental prefixes at this stage.
  2. Alteration-roll for a substantial flat elemental prefix plus local attack speed. If it has only one excellent mod, an Augmentation fills the other affix type randomly; it does not guarantee the missing stat.
  3. Regal a magic result only when its existing damage and speed are worth keeping. Regal adds one random affix without rerolling them.
  4. Bench-craft a different flat element into an open prefix. A two-mod magic item plus one Regal cannot already have all three prefix slots filled. The extra Regal mod can be unhelpful; it does not have to be removed for this budget finish.

Stop rule: use the resulting two-element, fast rare if it improves your character. Do not turn this budget method into a Divine-cost multimod craft. If repeated Alterations give nothing useful, pause and use drops, Rog or essences rather than chasing a sunk cost.

Reference: basic currency crafting, natural and crafted claw modifiers.

3. Your first mapping chest: preserve links, build usable defenses Early maps

Bring: an uncorrupted evasion or armor/evasion chest with a usable 4/5-link. Prioritize a found 5-link over a perfect unlinked base; keep evasion while relying on the four-slot Evasion Mastery. There is no ilvl-86 requirement for your first maps.

  1. First ask whether it needs rerolling. A dropped rare with life, two helpful suffixes and one open craft can already be finished. If only one resistance is wrong, use the Harvest conversion recipe below instead of erasing the item.
  2. For a linked base with bad stats: use a Screaming-or-better Essence of Greed for guaranteed flat life, or a resistance essence if that resistance is the urgent problem. These change affixes while keeping the links. The same essence has different effects on weapons, armor and jewelry, so inspect its chest line.
  3. Keep a balanced result. Roughly 70–100+ life and a couple of meaningful resistances is a useful early direction. Suppression is valuable when it closes your actual gap; do not require life, three resistances and suppression together, since those suffixes compete.
  4. Finish the missing half. Good suffixes and an open prefix can take crafted life; good life and an open suffix can take resistance. If the item is already an upgrade, equip it and make the next attempt on another base.
  5. Separate links from affixes. If you must link a worthwhile chest, use quality first when available and set a Fusing budget. After unlocking the recipes, the guaranteed bench costs are 150 Fusings for five links or 1,500 for six, with sockets handled separately. Check the bench recipe before farming its cost; random Fusings have no fixed success count.

Stop rule: a defensive 5-link is a strong early mapping result. Do not strip life and resistance just to wear six links, or spend 1,500 Fusings on a temporary base by default. For a durable later project, choose a suitable high-level base; ilvl 86 opens top natural body-armor suppression tiers.

4. Rog: build suppression armor without burning your essence stock Expedition

Bring: Order Artifacts and Scrap Metal from Expedition. Rog improves items bought from his own stock; you cannot hand him your equipped chest. Use Expedition Threads/logbooks as available.

  1. Inspect bases before refreshing. Favor dexterity-based chest/helmet/gloves/boots with a promising suppression or resistance suffix, or good life/defense prefixes. Check requirements, item level and the stats already present.
  2. Decide which half is worth keeping. Suppression plus two useful resistances? Favor offers that improve or reroll prefixes. Already excellent life/defenses? Favor suffix work instead. Read the exact offer; a whole-item reroll can erase both halves.
  3. Take tier upgrades when they improve useful affixes. “Reroll values” changes numbers within a modifier's tier; it is not the same as upgrading that tier. Do not preserve a high-tier stat the build does not use.
  4. Skip a destructive offer if allowed, or take the item when it is already useful. Consecutive skips are restricted. Do not assume a future offer will rescue an item you deliberately break.
  5. Finish the resulting rare at your bench. A useful open prefix or suffix can be better than a random sixth mod. Link the finished base separately if necessary.

Stop rule: keep a real life/suppression/resistance improvement, even without perfect tiers. Rog is an alternative resource pool, not a guaranteed source of all-T1 gear.

Mechanics: Rog's offers and skip restrictions, early mapping upgrades. Apply the 3.30 offer changes summarized above.

5. Harvest: distinguish a targeted repair from a full reroll Lifeforce

Bring: Lifeforce and a rare spare item for reforges. Use the Horticrafting Station; check the current recipe wording and price before confirming.

  1. Repair a resistance on an otherwise good item: choose the conversion from its unwanted single-element resistance to the element you lack. For example, convert surplus fire to cold. Check eligibility and mod-family conflicts; a fractured modifier cannot be changed. This is different from “Reforge Cold,” which rerolls the item.
  2. Build a chest from a bad linked rare: Life reforge costs 75 Wild Lifeforce in the listed pool. It forces a life-tagged modifier, not a high flat-life roll, good suppression or useful resistances. Inspect after each attempt, then finish an open slot if the result works.
  3. Try a spare claw: Lightning or Cold reforges cost 50 matching Lifeforce; Speed costs 150 Vivid. These target a modifier tag while rerolling the remaining affixes. An elemental tag can land on resistance rather than flat attack damage. Use a small batch and keep only complete improvements.
  4. Build a plain belt/ring toward chaos resistance: Chaos reforge costs 100 Vivid. Verify that item's chaos-tagged pool first, then look for useful life and remaining suffixes. An influenced base can add unwanted tagged possibilities. This is not an upgrade button for an already good life/resistance item.

Stop rule: convert the resistance when that is the only problem. Reforge only a spare whose existing affixes you are prepared to lose. Expensive add/remove crafts are a separate project; old “reforge keeping all prefixes/suffixes” tutorials are not this recipe.

Current recipe text and costs: Horticrafting Station list. Tag behavior: Harvest mechanics.

6. Fossils: useful when you already have Delve supplies Optional

Bring: fossils, matching Chaotic Resonators, and a spare eligible rare base. These resonators reforge rare items; do not try to apply them to a normal or magic base. Fossils bias modifier weights; they do not guarantee the affix or tier you want.

  1. Life-first chest: put a Pristine Fossil in a one-socket Chaotic Resonator and apply it to the spare linked rare. It favors life and blocks defense-tagged explicit rolls. The armor's base defenses remain, but this is not the method for rolling large local increased armor/evasion prefixes.
  2. Defense-first chest: a Dense Fossil favors defense modifiers but blocks life-tagged rolls. That can serve a deliberately different plan; it does not roll the high-life chest recommended here. Do not combine Dense and Pristine expecting both benefits.
  3. Two-element claw: Frigid + Metallic in a two-socket Chaotic Resonator favors cold/lightning while blocking fire/physical tagged rolls. Look for good cold/lightning damage and speed, then bench the missing legal stat. You are not rolling a tri-elemental fire/cold/lightning weapon with this combination.

Stop rule: use supplies you have, inspect each result, and stop at a workable item. Do not divert the whole early Atlas into farming resonators if essences, Rog or Harvest already solve the slot.

Fossil effects: Pristine, Dense, Frigid, Metallic.

7. A useful found fracture changes the recipe When found

Bring: a suitable fractured base you actually found. Do not budget around buying one in SSF. The locked modifier must be useful on that base and strong enough to justify the project.

  1. Claw with fractured attack speed: use a flat-element essence, seek another useful element and an open prefix, then finish with a legal flat-damage craft. The fracture keeps the speed through ordinary essence reforges.
  2. Claw with fractured flat cold/lightning: choose an essence of a different element. You now retain one damage roll while forcing another; look for natural speed or an open suffix to craft it. Do not choose an essence whose modifier conflicts with the fracture.
  3. Chest with fractured suppression: use Greed for life or a resistance essence, then seek complementary suffixes. The fracture reduces the number of stats you must hit together; it does not guarantee the rest.
  4. Check rarity before applying the essence. A fractured magic base may need a Regal to become rare for a Screaming+ reforge. Do not assume an essence accepts magic items, or Scour without understanding that the fractured mod remains.

Stop rule: spend better essences only on a useful fracture, not a bad locked mod on a prestigious base. Avoid trying to manufacture an expensive fracture as an early-map prerequisite.

References: fractured item behavior, essence targeting rules.

8. Keep chest suffixes; reroll prefixes for life Later maps

Bring: an eligible rare body armor with worthwhile suffixes, a Searing Exarch Ember, and a few Eldritch Chaos Orbs. This is a later finishing method, not a first-map requirement.

  1. Secure suffixes worth preserving first, such as suppression plus needed elemental/chaos resistances. If an open prefix and bench life already make it good, finish there before spending rarer currency.
  2. Use an eligible non-corrupted armor item, without Shaper/Elder/Conqueror or synthesised incompatibilities. Eldritch implicits belong on body armor, helmet, gloves or boots; this recipe does not apply to your claw or shield.
  3. Make Searing Exarch dominant. An Exarch implicit with no Eater implicit is sufficient; if both exist, Exarch must have the higher implicit tier. Equal tiers are not dominance. Read the item's actual state before using the orb.
  4. Use one Eldritch Chaos Orb. Under Exarch dominance it rerolls prefixes and retains suffixes. Check for useful life/defenses and an open prefix for the final craft. It can fill the prefix side; an open slot is not promised.
  5. Repeat only within your preset budget, then craft the missing prefix if possible. Improve the implicits later. Adding/upgrading an Eater implicit can change dominance, so verify it again before any further Eldritch crafting.

Critical distinction: Eater dominance rerolls suffixes, which would destroy the half you intended to keep. An ordinary Chaos, essence or unprotected Harvest reforge is not a substitute for this operation. This route needs no Divine-cost full-suffix lock.

Currency behavior: Eldritch Chaos Orb. Applied armor method: suffix-first body armor.

9. Recombination: salvage donors, with a separate budget Advanced alternative

Bring: spare compatible donors and the unlocked Kingsmarch Recombinator. Treat both inputs as expendable. This is a way to assemble good mods from drops, not a guaranteed upgrade to equipped gear.

  1. Save complementary donors: one fast claw with a strong cold prefix, another with strong lightning, or armor carrying useful suppression/resistance modifiers. Prefer matching usable bases so either output base suits the character.
  2. Check both item levels and legal mod pools. A donor is useful only if the intended result and base can support your follow-up crafts; do not use a low-level base expecting the output level to average upward.
  3. Choose the available recombination mode deliberately. Predictable recombination lets you select desired modifiers and displays a success chance. Unpredictable recombination selects from the pooled mods; an old guide's percentages are not a promise for this version.
  4. Attempt only on surplus donors, inspect the complete result, then finish an open affix normally. Keep successful intermediates. Do not add costly filler mods or strip everything to one-mod donors merely because an old formula recommended it.

3.30 guardrails: sparse-prefix/suffix Unpredictable combinations were weakened; corrupted inputs are disallowed. Check the current UI and make a donor-loss budget before trying to combine multiple premium rolls.

10. Later claw finishing: protect excellent prefixes, then seek speed Expensive

Bring: a spare rare claw with three excellent elemental prefixes, an open suffix, the full-prefix-lock recipe, its displayed materials, and Harvest resources. Compare the finished result in PoB before paying.

  1. Confirm all three damage prefixes deserve preservation. Three mediocre elements do not justify this route. A missing cheap speed craft may already finish a perfectly usable claw.
  2. Bench Prefixes Cannot Be Changed; it occupies a suffix. Use the full lock if the goal is to protect all three prefixes. A one-/two-prefix lock is not blanket protection for the whole prefix side.
  3. Harvest-reforge Speed after confirming the ordinary base's speed pool. The full lock preserves the prefixes for this supported operation; suffixes reroll, including removal of the lock. Speed tier and the number of resulting suffixes remain random.
  4. If speed is useful and a suffix remains open, craft crit or another appropriate missing stat. If the suffixes fill, stop and reassess: there is no free guaranteed annul. A further protected reforge requires space to reapply protection and another payment.

Stop rule: do not begin this for an early-map weapon. Never follow “lock → essence/fossil spam” as if those currencies were additive protected upgrades. Use the bench's current interaction rules and simulate the exact sequence on Craft of Exile, checking that its data matches the live patch.

Interactions: metamod behavior; reforge Speed. Full-lock cost and unlock changes are listed at the top of this tab.

What good gear looks like at each stage

Follow the same foundation throughout: a fast elemental claw, a shield, and life-based armor with evasion. Pure evasion or armor/evasion bases keep the four-slot Evasion Mastery working. Choose items that solve your current weakness; these are approximate shopping directions for SSF, not mandatory rolls on every slot.

How to read resistance totals: “50% combined elemental resistance” means the sum of fire, cold and lightning on that item; for example, +25% fire and +25% lightning. It does not mean +50% to each. A +10% to all elemental resistances modifier contributes 30 percentage points to this sum. Chaos resistance is separate. Your real goal is 75% displayed fire, cold and lightning resistance on the character sheet after Kitava; balance the three individually instead of chasing a large total in the wrong element.

Explore the equipment panel: hover, focus or tap a slot to inspect one possible item for that milestone. Rare item names are illustrative; their modifiers use real stat types with at most three prefixes and three suffixes. These are individual examples, not a fully resistance-balanced loadout or a list to copy exactly. The guidance below each panel explains what matters.

Flasks, tinctures and jewels: tinctures occupy flask slots, so each example belt has exactly five slots in total. Magic flasks and tinctures allow one prefix and one suffix; regular, Abyss and cluster jewels allow two of each. Jewel panels show candidates for available sockets, with optional future upgrades labeled separately.

Gem placement: colored sockets and gold links appear over the equipment. Inspect a slot for the full gem names and setup notes. A line joins gems that need to be linked; separate dots are independent skills or spare gems being leveled. You can move a whole group to another item with enough matching sockets and links. Gem order within a linked group does not affect the supports.

Example references: claw modifier ranges, evasion helmet modifiers, evasion boot modifiers, Nimis, Mageblood, flasks, tinctures, jewels, cluster jewels, Watcher's Eye.

Before replacing an item: check links and colors, life, all three resistance caps, attributes, hit chance and mana cost. Useful sockets can outweigh better affixes during the campaign. On rare armor and jewelry, an open prefix can allow bench-crafted life; an open suffix can allow a missing resistance or attribute. Most items accept only one bench craft.
